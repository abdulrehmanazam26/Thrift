# Campaign imagery

The built-in ImageGen tool generated the original editorial campaign concept. The selected project asset is `public/images/campaign.webp`, with responsive WebP variants in `public/images/responsive/`. The original is retained as `public/images/campaign-source.png`. This is brand mood photography, not a representation of inventory. Catalog stock photography sources are recorded in `IMAGE-CREDITS.md`.

Exact generation prompt:

> Use case: photorealistic-natural
> Asset type: original editorial campaign hero photograph for THRIFT VAULT, a pre-loved fashion website; brand editorial mood only, not actual merchandise.
> Create exactly ONE landscape image, 1536x1024 pixels, 3:2 aspect ratio.
> Scene/backdrop: a neutral concrete industrial studio with subtly textured walls and floor.
> Subject: exactly two young adult fashion models wearing oversized vintage washed charcoal and muted olive workwear with worn denim. Relaxed, confident editorial poses; natural anatomy and realistic faces.
> Style/medium: photorealistic premium contemporary streetwear magazine campaign, authentic textured film photography with fine grain, natural skin texture, tactile faded cotton and denim, understated imperfect realism.
> Composition/framing: wide eye-level photograph; both people grouped toward the RIGHT HALF of the frame, full or three-quarter body, heads completely visible. Keep the LEFT HALF spacious and largely empty, dark neutral concrete in shadow with low visual detail, suitable for large overlay typography to be added later. Coherent single photographic scene.
> Lighting/mood: dramatic soft sunlight from the right illuminating the models, gentle falloff into the dark left-side negative space; rich soft shadows, restrained editorial mood.
> Color palette: mostly desaturated neutrals, washed charcoal, muted olive, faded denim, concrete gray.
> Constraints: no text anywhere, no typography, no lettering, no logos, no brand marks, no watermarks, no UI, no borders, no collage, no extra people. Do not render the brand name. Generate one image only, no variants.

The Sites skill informed the visual direction and preview/QA workflow. The user's explicit Next.js and local-folder requirements determined the framework and delivery path. No Sites registration, synchronization, or public hosting was performed. Full project code lives in the requested preloved directory.
